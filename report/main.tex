\begin{abstract}
This paper studies the relationship between statutory minimum-wage policy, labour productivity, and consumer-price inflation in Portugal from the introduction of the national minimum wage in 1974. The long-run historical analysis is combined with an exposure-based regional and sectoral design intended to identify the dynamic pass-through of minimum-wage-induced labour-cost shocks into consumer prices. The empirical design separates statutory wage changes from productivity growth and prior inflation, exploits heterogeneity in minimum-wage coverage, and controls for taxes, energy, imported inflation and macroeconomic regimes.
\end{abstract}
