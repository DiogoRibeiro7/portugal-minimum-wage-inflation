\section{The limits of regional identification}
\label{sec:robustness}

The obvious way to move from the accounting of Section~\ref{sec:results} to a
causal statement about prices is to exploit the autonomous regions, which may
set a minimum wage above the mainland's. This section reports why that design
cannot be carried in Portugal. We present it as a result, because the reasons
are specific and checkable rather than a general complaint about power.

\subsection{How much variation actually exists}

Under a specification with region-by-category and calendar-time fixed effects,
what identifies a coefficient is the set of region-months in which the statutory
change differs from the mainland's. Counting them is not the same as counting
observations, and the difference is large.

The Azores contribute one month. Their supplement is proportional, so
$\Delta \log MW$ in the Azores equals $\Delta \log MW$ on the mainland exactly
in every month after the supplement took effect; the region adds rows to the
panel and not one degree of freedom. Madeira contributes one month per act.
Over the estimation window, and after excluding the years for which Madeira
legislated but the act is not yet in our register, \IdentifyingEvents{}
region-months diverge, across \IdentifyingRegions{} region.

We stress the direction of this problem. A gap in the register is not neutral:
if a region legislated in a year whose act is missing, its wage appears frozen
against a rising national one, which is indistinguishable from a deliberate
freeze and enters the estimation as a policy shock that never happened. The
pipeline therefore requires known-incomplete years to be declared, and marks the
transitions into and out of them unestimable rather than treating them as
observed changes.

\subsection{Why conventional inference cannot be trusted here}

Cluster-robust standard errors are justified asymptotically in the number of
clusters, and Portugal has few regions. That conventional inference over-rejects
in this situation, and that a wild cluster bootstrap corrects it, is established
\citep{cameron2008bootstrap}. In simulation with seven clusters and a
true null, the conventional test rejects at the five per cent level about twenty
per cent of the time, while a restricted wild cluster bootstrap holds its size.
With so few clusters the bootstrap can enumerate the entire space of sign
vectors, so its $p$-value is exact rather than simulated.

This is not a refinement. It is the difference between a result and an artefact,
and Table~\ref{tab:regional-design} shows it on the actual data.

Conventional inference calls \ClusteredHighlySignificant{} of the
\HorizonsEstimated{} horizons significant at the one-tenth of one per cent
level. The bootstrap rejects none of them at five per cent: its smallest
$p$-value is \MinBootstrapP{}. Had we reported only the third column, the paper
would have claimed substantial pass-through of minimum-wage costs into consumer
prices within months of implementation.

The magnitudes are the second reason to distrust them. The largest coefficient
is \PeakCoefficient{} at \PeakHorizon{}~months, which would imply prices rising
by more than the wage shock itself. An estimate identified by
\IdentifyingEvents{} events in one small, remote, tourism-intensive region is
picking up whatever else was happening in Madeira, and the bootstrap is
detecting exactly that.

\subsection{The alternative design, and why it also falls short}

If regional variation is unavailable, the remaining variation is across
consumption categories facing a common national change. Rather than impose an
exposure measure -- which would require minimum-wage coverage by region and
industry jointly, and no Portuguese source publishes it, and whose shift-share
construction carries assumptions of its own \citep{goldsmithpinkham2020bartik}
-- the differential
response can be estimated directly, one coefficient per category.

That design cannot carry calendar-time fixed effects, because a national shock
does not vary across regions within a month; time effects would absorb it
entirely. It therefore identifies each category relative to the others and is
exposed to anything else moving national prices at the same time. The estimated
ranking is partly plausible: food and, next, restaurants and accommodation sit
at the top, and accommodation and food services is the activity with by far the
highest measured minimum-wage coverage. But clothing and footwear returns a
large negative coefficient that survives category-specific linear trends, which
points to a break or a rebasing in that series rather than to any price
response. Until that is diagnosed the ranking is not reportable, because
whatever corrupts one category may be moving the others less visibly.

\subsection{What would change the conclusion}

Three things, in descending order of value. Registering Madeira's acts for
2010--2018, 2021 and 2022, each from primary law, would roughly quadruple the
identifying events. Minimum-wage coverage published by NUTS~II region and
economic activity jointly would permit the exposure design the literature
favours. And a longer regional price series at consumption-purpose detail would
allow pre-trend testing over a window long enough to be informative. None of
these is unobtainable; none is available now.
