\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The Portuguese minimum wage has had two distinct lives. For its first four
decades it lost ground: inflation eroded its real value faster than statutory
increases restored it, and it fell far behind an economy whose output per worker
more than doubled. Measured against productivity it reached barely a third of
its introductory value. Only in the last decade has that reversed, with the
policy residual turning persistently positive from 2015 and the real floor
regaining its 1974 level around 2017.

That is a descriptive account, and we have been careful to keep it one. The
residual measures what the wage floor did relative to a productivity-and-
inflation benchmark; it does not measure what the wage floor caused. We have
also shown, rather than asserted, why the natural causal design is unavailable:
the Azorean supplement is proportional and contributes no independent timing,
Madeira is the sole source of genuine regional divergence, and with the small
number of Portuguese regions the conventional inference that would make such a
design look conclusive rejects a true null far too often. On the actual data,
of the estimates significant at the one-tenth of one per cent level under
clustered standard errors, a wild cluster bootstrap leaves one, and a step-down
correction across the seven horizons leaves none.

We also tested that diagnosis against itself. The earlier version of this work
named Madeira's unregistered acts as the most valuable thing to obtain and
predicted that recovering them would roughly quadruple the identifying
variation. Recovering them tripled it, and the estimates became far more
plausible --- the peak coefficient fell from above one, which would have had
prices rise by more than the shock, to about a fifth of that --- without becoming
significant. That is the outcome the diagnosis implied: the binding constraint
is one treated region, not too few dates.

The usual answer to thin policy variation is a shift-share exposure measure, and
we built one rather than declaring the ingredients unavailable, which an earlier
version of this work did wrongly. Regional industry composition is published and
the national minimum-wage bite by activity is recoverable, so the measure
exists; it simply does not vary much, spanning \ExposureSpreadPP{}~percentage
points across the \ExposureRegions{} NUTS~II regions. That is a fact about how
evenly
the Portuguese wage floor binds across industries, and it is the more useful
finding, because no additional data collection of the kind usually proposed
would change it.

We think the honest conclusion is that Portugal is a poor setting for
identifying minimum-wage price pass-through from regional variation, and that
saying so is more useful than a weakly identified estimate. The country's
statutory history is, however, unusually well documented in primary law, and the
long-run record it supports is informative in its own right: a wage floor whose
real generosity has moved by a factor of two over fifty years, in both
directions, is a substantial object of study.

The series, the regional schedules, the price panel and the pipeline that builds
them are released with this paper, along with the exposure measure and the
guards that refuse to build it where it would not be predetermined. Registering
the Madeira acts that remain outside the series, and obtaining the minimum-wage
bite measured by region rather than assumed constant within industry, would be
the two most valuable next steps for anyone wishing to revisit the causal
question.
